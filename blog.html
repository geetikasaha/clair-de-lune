On loss, change, and the quiet courage of beginning again.

on loss & change by Geetika Saha

Lost and Found

✦

Has it been long and you are trying to forget and get over a person who was once a close friend, companion, soulmate, childhood buddy, enemy or family? Do you feel the person who was once close to your heart is now far away, as if you've lost her/him? Here is my theory on why what happens and how to deal with it.

Think about it — will you ever try and wake a dead man? Losing people can be really hard but you know what is harder: it is not wanting to have them back in life. It is actually tough to stop your mind which expects more new beautiful memories of that person. Here is one simple truth you need to tell yourself — the person you want back has lost existence, my friend. Yes, it is the same face, same voice, same address — but this person from inside is not the same with whom you bonded. Wasn't that the main reason you had to lose them? The aura plays an important role, and the aura has changed. The aura that merged with yours has disappeared into oblivion. The inside, the soul, the essence you miss just does not exist now. Don't you think it is similar to trying to wake a dead?

It is sad — people change and you turn into a zilch factor in their life. That is a bitter truth of life, but how do we find hope here? Is there hope? Does time really hold a doctor's degree? Can it really heal?

I will try to share an experience and let's see where we get. I had a really close friend who was my childhood besty. Somehow she was no longer the person with whom I felt the way I used to. I stopped confiding my secrets in her, we stopped sharing, she chose a different path — and that is how we stopped being best friends. The catch is: I have a best friend now, and I love her. I will break this down for you — the aura you blend in with, the soul you fall for, the bonding you miss — it can return in your life, but with a different face, a different voice, and it can fill in the empty spot.

We as human beings are very impatient creatures. If we lose something we either want to replace it by getting a new one, or we try to find the lost one. Some of us over-thinkers might as well lose sleep because we lost a slipper. On a serious note — do you ever find that thing in a place you have tried searching a gazillion times? No, right? You merely waste an essential part of your energy in doing so. And even when you urgently want to find a substitute, do you think twice? Do you ask yourself if you need what you lost right now?

What I am trying to say is that your impatience leads you to wrong decisions and choices. Wait — because if you do not, you might even lose the chance to find what you lost in a new face. Don't keep digging the older hole; it will grow so deep that you won't ever be able to come out of it. You will be stuck in the hole believing that you want that face, that voice back. Give yourself a reality check: you just want to feel the way you used to feel with that person. If you are hell-bent to get that combination of face and aura, you are playing on a thin probability. Understand the non-existence of existence.

Don't even feel jealous when you are replaced — no one can take away the experience you have had with them, as it is unique. That memory and experience still belongs to just you. Be happy you had it. Your memories are about the aura and the soul, not the face — so you shall have it for life.

There can be many possibilities and negatives where we feel the lack of hope, but always know there is an equal number of positives waiting to be found. Life works on balance, after all. Make your theories and talk to yourself. Be the cool kind of crazy.
